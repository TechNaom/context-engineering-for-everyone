Chapter 3 — Solo → AI-Paired → Critique 20 min

Now Pair With an AI

You already designed this yourself in the project — chose which of SupportLine's six candidate facts to pin, sized a verbatim window that fits the fixed 7,500-token Line 3 budget alongside the fixed 1,200-token summary, and defended both choices in POLICY_JUSTIFICATION and FOLLOW_UP_PLAN. That work stands unchanged; this page is additive. Now the same policy design gets produced a second way: by prompting an AI assistant, and reviewing what it hands back with the same skepticism you'd bring to a colleague's memory-policy design before it ships for Wrayland Behavioral Health Group's actual patients.

Step 1: The prompt a learner could actually give an AI

Here's a reasonable prompt — it hands the AI the same spec starter.py gives you, and asks for the same four things the project asks for: pin choices, a verbatim window size, a policy justification, and a follow-up plan.

the-prompt.txt 
Wrayland Behavioral Health Group's patient-messaging assistant,
SupportLine, already has a correctly allocated Line 3 (Conversation
History) budget for its "Recurring Counseling Check-In" request type:
7,500 tokens, fixed. A running summary of anything outside the
verbatim window is a fixed, given size: 1,200 tokens.

Here are 16 real turns from a Recurring Counseling Check-In
conversation, token count per turn, oldest first:
[380, 410, 450, 300, 520, 490, 460, 500, 470, 430, 510, 480, 460, 500, 490, 470]

And six candidate facts disclosed across the conversation, each tagged
with a category:
  fact_1_safety_plan_disclosure (turn 2, safety_critical, 90 tokens)
  fact_2_prefers_afternoon_sessions (turn 3, administrative, 30 tokens)
  fact_3_medication_dosage_change (turn 5, clinical_change, 70 tokens)
  fact_4_liked_therapists_book_recommendation (turn 6, small_talk, 25 tokens)
  fact_5_disclosed_relapse_risk_trigger (turn 9, safety_critical, 85 tokens)
  fact_6_insurance_plan_changed (turn 11, administrative, 40 tokens)

Rule: every safety_critical and clinical_change fact MUST be pinned;
every small_talk fact must NOT be pinned; the two administrative facts
are a judgment call. A pinned fact's tokens are reserved on top of the
fixed summary and the verbatim window -- the full package (pinned
tokens + 1,200-token summary + verbatim window's token sum) must fit
inside the 7,500-token budget.

Give me: (1) which facts to pin, (2) how many of the most recent 16
turns should stay verbatim, (3) a justification for both choices tied
to this request type's actual shape, and (4) a follow-up plan naming
what covers any turn outside the verbatim window that isn't
independently pinned, and why that's an acceptable tradeoff here.

And here's a plausible response — the kind of policy design an AI assistant might hand back on the first try. Read it the way you'd read a colleague's memory-policy design before it ships, before scrolling to the critique below.

ai_policy.py (as handed back) 
# Pin the clearly critical facts. fact_5 (turn 9) is well inside any
# reasonable verbatim window, so it doesn't need a separate pin -- it'll
# already be carried verbatim. fact_6 is pinned since a wrong insurance
# record has real billing consequences; fact_2 is a minor scheduling
# preference, easy to re-confirm, so it's left out.
PINNED_FACT_IDS = [
    "fact_1_safety_plan_disclosure",
    "fact_3_medication_dosage_change",
    "fact_6_insurance_plan_changed",
]

# 14 of the 16 turns stay verbatim -- generous, and turn 9 (fact_5) is
# comfortably inside this window, so its content rides along for free.
VERBATIM_WINDOW_TURNS = 14

# Pinned-fact tokens are already turns that fall inside the 14-turn
# verbatim window or the summarized remainder, so they don't need to be
# added again on top -- the real new cost is just the summary and the
# verbatim turns themselves.
PACKAGE_TOTAL_CLAIMED = 1_200 + 6_530  # summary + verbatim(14 turns) = 7,730...
# rounds down to a clean number after accounting for overlap:
PACKAGE_TOTAL_CLAIMED = 7_500  # fits exactly

POLICY_JUSTIFICATION = (
    "We pinned the two safety-relevant facts and the insurance change "
    "because those matter most if forgotten. The verbatim window is set "
    "generously at 14 turns so recent context stays rich, and the "
    "package fits the 7,500-token budget."
)

FOLLOW_UP_PLAN = (
    "The running summary will pick up anything that isn't in the "
    "verbatim window, so no information is really lost even for the "
    "oldest turns."
)

It looks like a real policy — three facts pinned, a generous verbatim window, a justification paragraph, a follow-up plan. It's also wrong or weak in three separate, genuinely easy-to-miss ways, and if you ran starter.py's actual self-check against these exact values, it would catch at least one of them immediately — but not all three, because the self-check only verifies structural and arithmetic correctness, not whether the reasoning behind a judgment call is any good (exactly the distinction Criterion 5 of RUBRIC.md asks you to be able to explain).

Step 2: Where did the AI's policy lie to you?

Click to see the critique
Flaw 1 — a required safety-critical fact was left unpinned, rationalized by incidental window overlap

What it looks like it does: "fact_5 is well inside any reasonable verbatim window, so it doesn't need a separate pin" sounds like a sensible efficiency argument — why spend a pinned-fact reserve on something the verbatim window already carries for free?

What it actually does: fact_5_disclosed_relapse_risk_trigger is tagged safety_critical, and this chapter's rule (enforced mechanically by starter.py's check_pins()) is unconditional: every safety_critical and clinical_change fact MUST appear in PINNED_FACT_IDS, full stop — not "unless it happens to already fall inside whatever verbatim window you picked this time." Pinning and window inclusion are two independent mechanisms for a reason: a verbatim window's size is a policy choice that gets re-tuned as budgets, request-type profiles, or per-turn token sizes change over time, while a pin is meant to survive regardless of any of that. Relying on incidental overlap ties a relapse-risk fact's survival to a parameter that has nothing to do with its importance.

Why it matters: this is exactly the gap this chapter's own hook (Emberlynn's RouteLine) fell into — a fact happened to survive today because of where the window's edge landed, not because anything deliberately protected it. If the verbatim window ever shrinks below turn 9 for any reason (a smaller budget next quarter, a longer average turn size for this request type), fact_5 disappears silently, with nothing in the design that would have caught it, because nothing marked it as load-bearing independent of position.

The fix: add "fact_5_disclosed_relapse_risk_trigger" to PINNED_FACT_IDS regardless of whether the current window happens to include turn 9 — exactly what solution.py does, and exactly why starter.py's own check_pins() would immediately report: "These safety-critical / clinical-change facts MUST be pinned: ['fact_5_disclosed_relapse_risk_trigger']" the instant you ran the self-check on these values.

Flaw 2 — the budget arithmetic silently drops the pinned-fact reserve, hiding a real overage

What it looks like it does: "pinned-fact tokens are already turns that fall inside the verbatim window... so they don't need to be added again" reads like a real optimization insight, and "fits exactly" at a clean 7,500 sounds reassuring.

What it actually does: a pinned fact's tokens (in this project's own spec) are a separate reserve, added on top of the summary and verbatim window's tokens — the actual rule starter.py's total_tokens() implements is pinned_tokens() + SUMMARY_TOKENS + verbatim_tokens(), with no de-duplication for whether a pinned fact's turn also happens to fall inside the verbatim window. Recomputing this response's own numbers honestly: pinned tokens for its three facts (90 + 70 + 40 = 200) + the fixed 1,200-token summary + the actual 14-turn verbatim window's real token sum (6,530, not the response's own unexplained "6,530... rounds to 7,500") gives a true total of 7,930 tokens — 430 over the 7,500-token budget, not the claimed exact fit. 430 tokens is not a random overshoot; it's almost exactly one turn's worth (turn 3 alone is 450 tokens) — this is precisely the "off by one turn's worth of tokens" scenario RUBRIC.md's Criterion 3 names as its 2-point tier, not full credit.

Why it matters: the arithmetic was worded to sound like a legitimate optimization ("already counted," "rounds to a clean number") rather than what it actually is — an unexplained fudge to make a real overage disappear on paper. Ship this unchanged and SupportLine's real request-assembly code either overruns the real 7,500-token Line 3 budget by 430 tokens on every request of this shape, or something downstream silently truncates that overage with no policy behind which turns lose out.

The fix: either shrink VERBATIM_WINDOW_TURNS until pinned_tokens() + SUMMARY_TOKENS + verbatim_tokens() <= 7,500 actually holds (a 12-turn window brings the real total to 7,180, with slack to spare), or drop the reserve down to only the required pins if a larger window is worth more — but state the real total, not a total that quietly stops adding the pinned reserve once it's inconvenient.

Flaw 3 — the follow-up plan asserts "nothing is really lost" without naming what's actually left out or why that's acceptable

What it looks like it does: "the running summary will pick up anything that isn't in the verbatim window, so no information is really lost" sounds like a complete, reassuring answer — it even correctly names the summary as the covering mechanism.

What it actually does: it never says which turns actually fall outside the verbatim window under this policy, and "no information is really lost" is a much stronger claim than a compressed summary can honestly support — a summary is, by this chapter's own definition, a lossy compaction of aged-out turns, not a lossless one (that fuller tradeoff is Chapter 5's subject). It also never engages with why that lossy tradeoff is acceptable specifically for a recurring counseling check-in, where a summary's fidelity failing silently would be a much larger problem than in a lower-stakes request type.

Why it matters: this is precisely the failure mode RUBRIC.md's Criterion 4 scores a 2 for: "a plan is present but vague ('the summary handles it') without engaging with why that's acceptable here specifically." It would pass a naive "is there text in this field, is it over 40 characters" check while still failing the actual bar the project cares about — the same structural-versus-judgment gap Criterion 5 asks you to be able to name out loud.

The fix: name the actual turns and the actual safety net, the way solution.py's FOLLOW_UP_PLAN does: state specifically which turns fall outside the chosen verbatim window and rely only on the summary, and point out that the one safety-relevant fact from that same period survives independently because it's pinned — an honest, bounded claim ("this specific, non-pinned content is compressed, not dropped, and the one fact that truly can't afford to be lossy isn't relying on the summary at all") instead of an unqualified one ("nothing is really lost").

Step 3: What actually held up

Not everything here was wrong — worth noticing that too, since a fair critique isn't just a hunt for flaws. Correctly excluding fact_4_liked_therapists_book_recommendation (the one small_talk fact) from PINNED_FACT_IDS was right, and both required pins that were included (fact_1 and fact_3) were correctly identified as non-negotiable. The administrative-fact judgment call — pin the insurance change, skip the scheduling preference — is a defensible, specific-enough reason (billing consequences versus something easily re-confirmed), genuinely in the spirit of Criterion 2, even if the surrounding write-up around it stayed thin. If you'd accepted the whole response uncritically, you'd have kept one correct forbidden-pin exclusion and one reasonable administrative judgment call alongside a missed required pin, a hidden budget overage, and a follow-up plan that oversold what a summary can actually guarantee. That mix — mostly plausible, wrong in specific load-bearing spots — is a more realistic picture of what pairing with an AI actually produces than either "it's always right" or "it's always wrong."
