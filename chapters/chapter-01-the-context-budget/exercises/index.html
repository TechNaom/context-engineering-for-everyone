Chapter 1 Exercises ~40 min, hands-on

Exercises: The Context Budget

These exercises use a second scenario, deliberately different from the lesson's Brackwater Home Internet/SignalDesk hook: Cobalt Home Security, a fictional home-security company. Its customer chat assistant, GuardLine, walks customers through alarm troubleshooting, pulls relevant knowledge-base articles, and can call a tool to check a customer's account and equipment record. Applying the five-line ledger to a fresh scenario is the point; recalling the lesson's answers by heart won't get you through these.

Exercise standard Eight tasks total. Five are marked production-gear — memory-fix evaluation, budget arithmetic, eviction-policy selection, lost-in-the-middle ordering, and context-health monitor design, not just concept recall.
How to run these Download starter.py, fill in every # TODO, then run python3 starter.py to see an automated score report. Compare against solution.py, which scores a perfect total.
starter.py solution.py (reference)

1 Map failures to ledger lines

Assign the correct ledger-line ID (e.g. L1) to five GuardLine failure descriptions — the bloated system prompt, the un-evicted knowledge-base hits, the unbounded transcript, the missing persistent recall, and the missing output reservation.

2 Ledger line dependency reasoning

For three pairs of ledger-line conditions, decide whether the first genuinely makes the second more likely, or whether the two are independent.

3 Production-gear Evaluate which fixes address Conversation History

Given four candidate fixes for GuardLine, decide which ones actually address the Conversation History line (summarization) versus which address a different line entirely (KB re-ranking, persistent recall, system-prompt trimming).

4 Production-gear Context budget arithmetic

Compute GuardLine's real conversation-history token budget and the maximum number of full turns it can hold, given an 8,000-token window and fixed budgets for system instructions, output, and grounding context.

5 Production-gear Memory eviction policy selection

GuardLine's conversation history is about to exceed its budget. Pick the most appropriate first eviction policy from four options.

6 Production-gear Lost-in-the-middle ordering decision

Pick where a critical fact should sit in assembled context, from four options, to make the model most likely to actually use it.

7 Production-gear Context-health monitor design

Name one concrete monitor (a specific metric plus a specific threshold or comparison) that would have caught GuardLine's growing budget risk before it caused a truncation-driven incident.

8 Production-gear Full-ledger completeness check

Confirm all five ledger lines were at least considered while working through Exercises 1–7 — the same completeness gate a real team runs before a context-engineered feature ships to production.
